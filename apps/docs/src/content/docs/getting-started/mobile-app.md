Stubwise has an official mobile app for iOS and Android, alongside [the web
app](/docs/getting-started/web-app/). It talks to the same instance and the
same account — think of it as a lighter, notification-first way in, not a
separate product.

## Installing

The app is v1 and internally distributed — there's no public App Store or
Play Store listing yet:

- **iOS**: you need a **TestFlight** invite from an administrator. Accept it,
  install the TestFlight app if you don't have it, then install Stubwise from
  there.
- **Android**: either a **Play internal testing** invite (same idea as
  TestFlight — accept it, install from Play), or a direct **APK** link an
  administrator shares with you. Installing an APK directly requires allowing
  installs from outside the Play Store on your device.

## Logging in

On first open, the app asks for three things:

- **Instance URL** — the domain of your organization's Stubwise (e.g.
  `stubwise.example.com`);
- **Email**;
- **Password**.

This is the exact same account you already use on the web. Logging in
registers this device with a dedicated access token (not the cookie the web
app uses), named `Mobile · <device name>` — it shows up under your personal
access tokens on the web, which is also where you'd revoke it if you lost the
phone. Logging out from the app revokes that same token.

## Notifications

Right after login, onboarding asks you to follow a few projects and to turn
on notifications. Turning them on is what actually matters: the real consent
for push is the **system permission prompt**, not a setting inside the app —
without it, no token is ever created and the channel stays silent by
construction, no matter what you do afterwards in Settings. If you skip it,
you can still open the app and check the inbox yourself; you just won't be
notified.

Once enabled, push notifications cover the same events your [inbox already
tracks](/docs/notifications/#when-the-ai-asks-a-question) — a question the AI
needs answered, a plan waiting for approval, a PR that's ready, a failed fix,
a proposal from an idle project's pulse, and more. Some arrive with quick
actions right on the notification (approve, run with the recommended option,
retry) that resolve the decision without opening the app; anything that needs
free text opens the app to answer.

You can turn push back off later from **Settings → Notifications** inside the
app — it's the mobile equivalent of the Slack DM opt-out on the web. Revoking
the OS-level notification permission from your phone's system settings has
the same effect: no permission, no token, no push.

## What you can do from the app

- **Inbox** — the decisions that need you: questions, plan approvals, ready
  PRs, failed jobs, backlog proposals. Same content as the web inbox, read
  and answered from your phone.
- **Projects** — the projects you follow, each with a plain-language summary
  of what's going on (jobs running, PRs waiting, backlog ready) rather than
  raw logs; tapping one opens its current job with a timeline told in words.
- **Backlog** — browse each project's backlog, tap **Continue** on an item
  that's ready to restart, capture a new idea as a quick note, or chat about
  one to refine it.
- **Docs** — search and browse a project's [autogenerated
  documentation](/docs/documentation/autogenerated/), or ask it a question
  directly.
- **Settings** — the push and followed-projects controls above, the instance
  you're connected to, your **interface language** (English/Italian, the same
  per-user setting as on the web), and log out.
